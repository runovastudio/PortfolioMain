On Games And Play

I don’t perceive games as a “product,” but rather as a space for thought.

I do not favor rigid constraints. Genres, boundaries, established structural habits, or assertions such as “this is how a game should or should not be” are frameworks I intentionally avoid. A game can take any form.

In many cases, a single conceptual premise is sufficient and everything else is defined by how that premise operates. My interest begins precisely there: how does something become “playable,” and why does it become playable? What constitutes the components of a game, and what should the subject of a game be? Or, more fundamentally, must a game have a subject at all? Is anything that can be played, a game?

In this sense, anything can be a game, frankly. However, allowing everything to become a game requires a certain intellectual and creative courage. I do not approach games as isolated intellectual properties detached from society, culture, art, or human context.

I am not attempting to fill a void. I am designing the void itself. I become the void. Rather than designing games as closed objects, I focus on designing the relationship between the game and the player. I am interested in how minimal structures can produce disproportionate outcomes.
