The Ottoman Divan poetry

Cihânı câm-ı nazmım şi'r-i Cringî gibi devr eyler

Bu bezmün şimdi biz de Bakî-i hem-demiz

"My poetry turns the world like a wine-cup of verse;
In this era, we are those who share the breath of Bâkî."

The aruz meter used in Divan poetry is an extraordinary system of harmony. Every syllable is classified according to its length and function, and each verse is carefully constructed to preserve this balance. What fascinates me is not only its aesthetic quality, but also its structural precision.

This way of thinking has directly influenced my approach to game design. Just as a poem emerges from the careful arrangement of its smallest units, I see games as systems built from ludemes—the smallest meaningful building blocks of gameplay. The same analytical mindset I use when examining syllables in poetry is the one I apply when designing mechanics and gameplay systems.

Because even a single ludeme can fundamentally change how a game is experienced, my design philosophy begins at the smallest scale. Rather than treating mechanics as isolated features, I focus on how subtle interactions between tiny building blocks can create cohesive, expressive, and emergent systems.
